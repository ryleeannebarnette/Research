JOIN THE LAB

Undergraduate research positions

No prior computational chemistry experience is required — most students in the lab learn Python, bash, and the relevant software on the job. What matters is curiosity and a willingness to get comfortable in the terminal.

HOW STUDENTS RAMP UP

Three stages, roughly one semester each

01

Learn the tools

Get comfortable with the Linux command line, Python for scientific computing, and the basics of running and reading DFT calculations under supervision.

02

Run real calculations

Take on a slice of the screening pipeline — running a batch of structures through part of the workflow and interpreting the results with a mentor.

03

Own a sub-project

Lead a defined piece of the research, often contributing to a manuscript, a conference poster, or one of the lab's open-source tools.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need coding experience already?

＋

No. Most students arrive knowing little to no Python or bash and learn both in their first semester through guided tasks and pair-programming with senior lab members.

Can this become a thesis or paid position?

＋

Yes — many students who stay beyond the first semester move into a paid research-assistant role or an honors thesis project, depending on department funding and your progress.

How much time does it take per week?

＋

Plan on 6–10 hours a week during the semester. This is flexible around your course schedule, but consistency matters more than total hours.

What year should I be in to apply?

＋

Sophomores and juniors are the most common starting point, since it gives time to ramp up and stay for multiple semesters, but strong first-years and seniors are also welcome to reach out.

Ready to apply?

Email a short note about your interest along with your current transcript or course list — no formal research statement needed.
