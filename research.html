RESEARCH INTERESTS

A multiscale route to photon-triplet crystals

Most photonic quantum-computing proposals rely on entangled photon pairs, produced by spontaneous parametric down-conversion. Far less is known about crystals that can split a pump photon into three entangled photons at once — a process with stricter symmetry and nonlinear-response requirements. Our group computationally identifies and characterizes candidate crystals for this process, and builds the software pipeline that makes the search tractable.

Why triplets are harder than pairs

A three-photon down-conversion event requires a crystal with the right third-order nonlinear susceptibility and phase-matching geometry — rare in nature, and expensive to check for computationally, one crystal at a time.

Structure ingestion & symmetry filtering

Candidate crystal structures are pulled from open structure databases and filtered by point-group symmetry — only non-centrosymmetric crystals can show the nonlinear response we need.

02

Electronic structure (DFT) & phonons

＋

Surviving candidates go through density-functional-theory relaxation and phonon calculations to confirm dynamical stability before any expensive optical-property calculation is attempted.

VASP / Quantum ESPRESSOphonopy
03

Nonlinear-optical response

＋

We compute third-order nonlinear susceptibility tensors and phase-matching conditions to test whether a stable, symmetry-allowed crystal can actually support three-photon down-conversion.

DFPTcustom tensor analysis in Python
04

ML-surrogate screening

＋

Because full first-principles calculations don't scale to thousands of candidates, we train surrogate models on the completed calculations to rank the remaining, uncalculated structures.

scikit-learnPython
05

Visualization & shortlist

＋

Top-ranked structures are modeled and visualized to check for growable, synthesizable geometries, producing a shortlist to hand to experimental collaborators.

CURRENT THREADS

What we're actively working on

  • Building a database of non-centrosymmetric crystals scored for third-order nonlinear response.
  • Improving the accuracy-vs-cost tradeoff of the ML surrogate that triages candidates before DFT.
  • Automating the DFT → phonon → nonlinear-response pipeline with reproducible bash workflows.
  • Modeling how lattice defects and temperature affect photon-triplet generation efficiency.
  • Collaborating with experimentalists to test the lab's top predicted crystal candidates.
  • Extending the screening methodology to layered and low-dimensional materials.
